## 1. Thomas Metzinger — the cheap falsifier, on his own data

*Route: mpe-project.info (project contact) · Hook: design 1 runs on the corpus he already collected.*

> Subject: Blind tradition-classification on the MPE corpus — has anyone run it?
>
> Dear Professor Metzinger,
>
> I've been working through The Elephant and the Blind and the MPE-92M paper. A question about a test I can't find in the literature: has anyone taken the pure-awareness reports, stripped the practitioners' tradition/lineage labels, and had blind raters (or a classifier) try to assign each report back to its tradition using only the core phenomenology?
>
> Above-chance classification would seem to falsify the strong "culture-free residue" reading directly; at-chance would be its best surviving evidence. If this hasn't been run, is the corpus in a state where an external collaborator could pre-register and run it?
>
> Advik Bhatt

## 2. Matthew Sacchet — cross-tradition adepts on existing infrastructure

*Route: meditation.mgh.harvard.edu · Hook: his program is the only one with the scanner pipeline for advanced practitioners.*

> Subject: Cross-tradition contentless-state neurophenomenology — planned?
>
> Dear Dr. Sacchet,
>
> Your jhana 7T and cessation EEG work is, as far as I can tell, the only pipeline in existence for scanning genuinely advanced practitioners. Is there any plan to extend it across contemplative traditions — e.g., Christian apophatic contemplatives or Sufi practitioners reporting contentless states — to test whether the "pure awareness" report class shares a neural signature across routes in, or only a shared description?
>
> Meditation quieting the DMN while psychedelics raise global connectivity suggests "same report, different implementation" — but within-meditation, cross-tradition comparison seems like the missing cell of the design. Is that on your roadmap, and if not, what's the blocker — funding or adept recruitment?
>
> Advik Bhatt

## 3. Ralph W. Hood Jr. — the circularity critique, from the source

*Route: UTC psychology faculty page · Hook: he's answered every other critic; ask him the sharpest version directly.*

> Subject: M-Scale and the Stace-circularity critique — your current best answer?
>
> Dear Professor Hood,
>
> The three-factor replication across your US, Iranian, and Chinese samples remains the strongest published support for the common core. The sharpest critique I've found (Breau & Gillis-Smith 2023) argues Stace's typology was built in dialogue with transnational Vedanta, so Stace-derived instruments recovering Stace-shaped factors is template-confirmation rather than discovery.
>
> What is your current best answer to that — and do you know of any attempt at a fully emic, non-Stace-derived instrument that still recovers the introvertive core? If none exists, would you consider that the decisive next study?
>
> Advik Bhatt

## 4. Ann Taves — the adversarial collaboration

*Route: religion.ucsb.edu/people/ann-taves · Hook: she's the constructive critic; invite the adjudicating design, not agreement.*

> Subject: Matched-induction, tradition-blind coding — would you co-design it?
>
> Dear Professor Taves,
>
> Your building-block framework seems to me the only position that could survive both a positive and a negative result in the following design: same induction protocol (meditative or clinical-psychedelic), practitioners recruited from different traditions, experience content coded blind to tradition. Strong prediction-from-tradition supports constructivism; weak prediction supports a recurrent core.
>
> As far as I can find, this — the direct Katz-vs-Forman adjudicator — has never been run cleanly. Is that right? And if a well-scoped version were funded, would you be interested in co-designing the coding framework so that neither camp could dismiss the result?
>
> Advik Bhatt
