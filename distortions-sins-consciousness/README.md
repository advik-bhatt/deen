# The Grammar of the Broken Mind

### Where cognitive distortions, the seven deadly sins, and every religion's catalogue of vice turn out to be — mostly — the same map, drawn by hands that sometimes copied each other

> **The ask, in his words.** *"Bring together every single overlap between every single cognitive distortion and the seven deadly sins and all of their parallels across each religion… every religion has parallels for the seven deadly sins, Shadrupu… We basically want to solve religion, psychology, and human consciousness. Fuck all the classifications. Everything is a gray area when it comes to psychology as well. OCD, ADHD, dyslexia: these are all just buckets and human constructs."*

## 0. Read this before anything else

**This is a synthesis, not a scripture-fetch.** The session's egress policy returned HTTP 403 for every primary-text host attempted — Sefaria, Quran.com, BibleGateway, Wisdomlib, sacred-texts, the Vatican site. **No agent read a single primary text in full this session.** Every verse quoted below comes from the model's trained knowledge of these texts, cross-checked against web-search snippets, then independently re-checked by a dedicated verifier agent. For texts this old and this stable that is a far smaller risk than it would be for frontier science — but it is not zero. **The single most likely defect in this document is a wrong chapter-and-verse number**, not a wrong idea. Every locus carries a confidence mark (§3). The verifier confirmed all the load-bearing ones; the handful it could not open are flagged in place.

**One error is pre-corrected, because everyone makes it.** The "seven deadly sins" are **not a list that appears anywhere in the Bible.** They are a patristic-to-medieval construction — Evagrius's eight thoughts (c. 375), Cassian's Latin transmission, Gregory the Great's reduction to seven (*Moralia in Job* XXXI, c. 590). Proverbs 6:16–19 ("six things the LORD hates, seven that are an abomination") is a *different* list — of outward acts (murder, false witness, sowing discord), only one of which ("haughty eyes") overlaps a capital vice — and it gets misquoted as the source constantly. This dossier keeps scripture and later systematization on separate shelves throughout.

**On "solving" religion, psychology, and consciousness — the honest verdict, up front.** We did not, and the strong version of the claim did not survive its own red team. What survives is narrower and more defensible, and it is stated here so it can't be lost in the enthusiasm below:

> At least seven independent civilizations converged on nearly the same short catalogue of ways a mind turns against itself, and twentieth-century cognitive science, starting over and refusing all the theology, landed on the same catalogue relabeled. **But that convergence is a mix of three different things, and the honest work is telling them apart:** (1) *documented copying* — Evagrius→Cassian→Gregory→Aquinas is one traceable lineage running back into Plato and the Stoics, and the Stoic→CBT link in particular is **inheritance, not rediscovery** (Albert Ellis built REBT explicitly on Epictetus, and both Ellis and Beck named Stoicism as cognitive therapy's acknowledged ancestor); (2) *shared vocabulary* — rāga, dveṣa, moha, avidyā recur near-verbatim across Hindu, Buddhist, Jain and Sikh texts because they drew on one Indic word-hoard; and (3) a genuine, *thinner-than-advertised residue* of independent convergence, of which the cleanest case is structural and nearly tautological (frustrated desire produces anger — Gītā 2.62 matching the CBT frustration→wrath sequence). The correct headline is therefore **"partly-shared vocabulary + family resemblance + one real transmission that is usually misfiled as convergence,"** not "independent discoveries of a single metaphysical object." §6 makes this case and then attacks it.

**His last instruction is the method, not a mood.** *"Fuck all the classifications. Everything is a gray area."* That is the spine. The one part of the grand thesis that is *strong* is precisely his intuition: **the number seven is not a natural kind — it is a cultural pack-size.** Evagrius counted eight; Gregory compressed to seven by editorial choice; the Jains carve four; the Sikhs five; the Ṣaḍripu six; Burns ten distortions; the pop-CBT worksheets fifteen. Each is a culture deciding where to draw cut-lines through one continuous field of maladaptive appraisal, and the lines fall in different places because the field has no seams of its own — exactly as OCD, ADHD and dyslexia are real phenomena with arbitrary fences. This dossier is a catalogue of maps, laid over each other until the territory shows through the gaps. It spends as much care on the gaps (§7) as on the overlaps (§1), because the gaps are where the honesty lives.

**The mechanism notes — the load-bearing claim of each row:**

1. **Pride.** A core self-schema ("I must be superior/admired") runs the mental filter selectively — admitting confirming data, screening disconfirming — and manufactures a chronically inflated self-appraisal. Vainglory is the *same* schema made conditional on an audience (a fortune-telling loop about one's reputation), which is why the tradition kept splitting one vice into an inward and an outward form. Beck's schema doing its filtering job *is* what the monastics called pride corrupting the *nous*.
2. **Greed.** Maps precisely onto the Stoic *epithumia* — irrational reaching for a supposed *future* good. A future-threat appraisal ("I will not be okay") drives accumulation as anxiety-management; the goods are never sufficient because the schema, not the bank balance, generates the threat. The *Daodejing* (ch. 46) states the mechanism cleanly: "no calamity greater than not knowing what is enough" — the distortion is in the appraisal "enough", not in the object.
3. **Lust/gluttony.** The Stoic *hēdonē*/*epithumia* axis — swelling toward a present good. The craving produces an automatic thought ("I need this") taken as fact (emotional reasoning); the satisfaction it predicts is a fortune-telling error the object never redeems. Two traditions (Vedic *kāma*, Jewish *yetzer*) insist the underlying drive is *good when channeled* — which the sin-frame structurally cannot hold. That is evidence the "vice" is a mis-calibration of a healthy appetite, not an evil in itself.
4. **Wrath — the cleanest vice↔mechanism fit in the table.** Anger fires when a rigid "should/must" about how others or reality *ought* to be is violated, plus a hostile-intent inference (mind-reading). Ellis built REBT on exactly this: the *demand*, not the event, generates the rage. And the Indic insight that *krodha* is downstream of *thwarted kāma* (Gītā 2.62) matches the CBT sequence exactly — frustrated desire + a violated "must" = wrath. This row is the strongest *non-diffusion* convergence in the whole document (see §6).
5. **Envy.** A comparison distortion: the mind selects an upward referent, discounts its own goods, inflates the rival's, reads the relation as zero-sum. The "fairness fallacy" supplies the grievance. Tellingly, the traditions least focused on interpersonal status (Sikhism's God-facing *haumai*, Jainism's soul-mechanics) simply *drop it* — suggesting envy is less a fundamental appraisal-error than a socially-scoped application of the self-schema.
6. **Sloth/dejection — the row where CBT out-describes every religion.** What Evagrius called *akēdia* and Beck calls depression are the same object: a self-sealing negative-appraisal loop whose behavioral output is withdrawal. Beck's cognitive triad (negative view of self, world, future) is a more precise anatomy of the noonday demon than the monastics achieved — and, decisively, the tradition that studied it *most* (Christian monasticism) still couldn't hold it stable in its own list, splitting and merging sadness/*acedia* for a millennium. That is exactly what you'd expect if it is a clinical syndrome being forced into a moral category.
7. **Attachment — where the Western and Indic maps stop being translations of each other.** *Abhiniveśa* (clinging to one's own continuance) is the death-anxiety schema. The traditions that name it locate the deepest "vice" not in wanting a thing but in the refusal to let anything, including the self, be impermanent. This has no seven-sins slot because the Christian scheme moralizes disordered *willing toward objects*, not the metaphysical error of treating the transient as permanent.
8. **Root misperception — the load-bearing row.** Buddhism, Yoga and Beckian CBT independently converge on the same structural claim the Western sin-lists never made: **affect-vices are secondary; the generator is a prior cognitive error** — a schema that mis-appraises reality and *then* produces craving and aversion as downstream symptoms. This is why CBT resembles Buddhism far more than it resembles the seven sins: both are diagnostic-therapeutic (uproot the misperception with *viveka-khyāti* or cognitive restructuring), not juridical. The Christian scheme's *absence* of any delusion-head is the single sharpest structural gap in the comparison, and it exposes the deepest disagreement of all: **does vice start in the will (Augustine) or the intellect (Buddha / Beck)?**

## 2. The two distortions under all the others

Two cognitive moves are not tied to any single vice; they underlie all of them, and every tradition names one or both.

**The first is emotional reasoning — taking a feeling as evidence of a fact ("I feel it, therefore it is so").** This is the universal engine. Every vice in the table runs on it: pride assents to "I am superior," greed to "I won't be okay without more," wrath to "they must not have done that," despair to "the future is hopeless." Strip out the false assent and the affect has nothing to stand on.

The Stoics gave this its most exact anatomy 2,300 years before Beck. For Chrysippus a *pathos* is not an irrational animal fighting reason — it *is* a defective judgment: the *hēgemonikón* (the ruling faculty) wrongly *assents* (*sunkatáthesis*) to an impression that some indifferent thing is genuinely good or bad, producing an "excessive impulse" beyond reason's control. His cure — withhold assent — is cognitive restructuring in Greek. **But here is where honesty forces a correction the first draft of this synthesis got wrong** (§9 logs it): this is *not* an example of independent minds converging. Ellis built REBT explicitly on Epictetus's "men are disturbed not by things but by their views of them," and both Ellis and Beck named Stoicism as cognitive therapy's acknowledged ancestor. The line runs Stoa → Ellis → the milieu Beck worked in. It is a *documented inheritance*. It should never be cited as proof that convergence happens without contact — it is the opposite. And a second caution: Beck's "emotional reasoning" is a *narrow* distortion (I-feel-X-so-X-is-true), while Stoic *assent* is a *general* theory of how every belief forms. Equating them makes the parallel look tighter than it is.

**The second is the reification of a separate, defending self** — the manufacture of an "I" that then has status to protect, goods to accumulate, and continuance to cling to. This appears under a different name in every tradition, and the ones that thought hardest about it name it as the master root: Patañjali's *asmitā*, the Hindu *ahaṅkāra* (I-maker), the Buddhist *sakkāya-diṭṭhi* rooted in *avijjā*, the Quranic *nafs* and *hawā*, Aquinas's *superbia-as-radix* — and most explicitly of all, the Sikh **haumai** (*hau* + *mai*, "I + me"), which the Gurus treat not as a sixth thief but as the disease the five thieves are secretions of: *attachment and lust are haumai clinging outward; anger is haumai frustrated; greed is haumai accumulating; pride is haumai admiring itself.* Beck's deepest layer is the same object: the core self-schema ("I am unlovable / helpless / superior") that runs the filter and generates every surface distortion.

The two are really one structure seen from two angles: **emotional reasoning is the mechanism; ego-reification is what the mechanism defends.** (The caveat, logged honestly: calling the Sikh *haumai* — a soteriological concept about the soul severed from God, healed by grace — "the same object" as Beck's experimentally-posited cognitive structure flattens a real ontological gap. It is a family resemblance, not an identity. §6.)

## 3. Tradition by tradition, in its own words

Each tradition gets its own vocabulary and its own primary-text loci. Confidence marks: **[H]** high (reference and content both certain), **[M]** medium (verify the exact numbering), **[L]** low (could not corroborate the page this session). The faculty model — *the psychology under the ethics* — is where the real cross-cultural disagreement lives, so each subsection states it explicitly.

### 3.1 Christianity — the capital vices (a construction, not a scripture)

There is no biblical "seven deadly sins." There are two distinct canonical lists, and conflating them is the standard error:

- **Evagrius Ponticus's eight *logismoi*** (*Praktikos* §6, c. 375) **[H]**: *gastrimargia* (gluttony), *porneia* (lust), *philargyria* (avarice), *lypē* (sadness), *orgē* (anger), *akēdia* (listlessness/the "noonday demon"), *kenodoxia* (vainglory), *hyperēphania* (pride). **John Cassian** carries the same eight into Latin (*Institutes* V–XII; *Conferences* 5) **[H/M]**.
- **Gregory the Great's seven** (*Moralia in Job* XXXI, ch. 45; §-numbering varies by edition, hence **[M]**): he sets *superbia* apart as *regina peccatorum* (queen of sins), then lists seven springing from it — vainglory, envy, anger, *tristitia* (sadness), avarice, gluttony, lust. **His actual edits: he added envy (Evagrius lacked it), merged vainglory toward pride, and kept sadness — folding *acedia* into *tristitia*.** The familiar *acedia*→"sloth" swap and the dropping of sadness happen *downstream* (the SALIGIA catechesis, Aquinas). *(The workflow prompt's own phrasing "merging sadness into acedia" is backwards; the verifier caught it — see §9.)*
- **Aquinas** (*Summa Theologiae* I-II q.84) **[H]**: names them *vitia capitalia* — "capital" from *caput*, **head/source**, not "deadliest" — wellsprings from which other sins flow. Pride is *principium omnis peccati*, quoting Sirach 10:13 **[M]** ("the beginning of pride is sin").

**Scriptural seedbeds** (real, but not the list): Galatians 5:19–21 (*ta erga tēs sarkos*, ~15 works of the flesh) **[H]**; Colossians 3:5 (greed = idolatry) **[H]**; Mark 7:21–22 (*dialogismoi*, "evil thoughts" from the heart — the bridge to Evagrius) **[H]**; 1 John 2:16 (the threefold concupiscence) **[H]**; and Proverbs 6:16–19 **[H]**, the "six/seven the LORD hates" — *a different list of acts*, confirmed NOT to be the deadly sins.

**Faculty model:** the *logismos* — an intrusive thought arising in the *nous* before consent (pre-sin; the danger is dwelling, not the arising). Behind the *logismoi* lie the *pathē*, mapped onto Plato's tripartite soul: *epithymētikon* (appetite → gluttony, lust, avarice), *thymikon* (spirited → anger, sadness, *acedia*), *logistikon* (reason → the subtler vainglory and pride). **The whole scheme is a Christian-monastic crystallization of Greek moral psychology.**

### 3.2 Judaism — one drive, not a list of sins

There is no Jewish seven deadly sins; reading one in distorts the tradition. It offers instead:

- **The single-drive model:** *yetzer hara* (evil inclination) vs *yetzer hatov* — Genesis 6:5, 8:21 **[H]** ("the *yetzer* of man's heart is evil from his youth"); the two *yetzarim* derived from the doubled *yod* of *va-yitzer* (Berakhot 61a) **[H]**. Crucially, Bereshit Rabbah 9:7 **[H]** calls the *yetzer hara* **"very good"** — "for without it no one would build a house, marry, or engage in commerce." The drive is raw energy to be channeled, not an evil to be extinguished. Its escalation runs from "a spider's thread to a cart-rope" (Sukkah 52a) **[H]**, and "the greater the person, the greater his *yetzer*" (Sukkah 52a) **[M]**.
- **The tightest short vice-list:** Pirkei Avot 4:21 **[H]** (numbered 4:28 in some editions), R. Elazar HaKappar — ***kin'ah, ta'avah, kavod*** (envy, desire, honor-seeking) "drive a person out of the world." Note this maps to three of the seven sins (envy, lust, pride) and *silently omits the other four*.
- **The mean:** Rambam, *Hilchot De'ot* 1:4 **[H]** — every trait should take the middle measure (openly imported Aristotle, mediated through Islamic *falsafa*). The two exceptions, *De'ot* 2:3 **[H]**: for **anger (*ka'as*) and pride (*gaavah*)** one must *not* take the mean but flee to the far extreme.
- **Mussar:** *Orchot Tzaddikim* (15th c.) **[H]** divides the moral life into 28 *she'arim* (gates), Gate 1 being pride.

**Faculty model:** the *yetzer* ("a forming/framing", same root as a potter shaping clay), seated in the *lev* (heart) — the biblical seat of will *and* thought, not a separate lower soul. Sin is *ḥet*, "missing the mark" — an **act**, not a false cognition. This is why Judaism has no delusion-head (row 8): the fault is in the aim, not the perception.

### 3.3 Islam — the *nafs* and the diseases of the heart (Quran-primary)

Per the ask, the Quran is the scripture; Al-Ghazālī's systematization is flagged as post-Quranic scholarship, not scripture.

- **The *nafs* in three states — a developmental ladder, not three vices:** *an-nafs al-ammāra bi's-sū'* ("the self that relentlessly commands evil", Q 12:53) **[H]**; *an-nafs al-lawwāma* ("the self-reproaching self / conscience", Q 75:2) **[H]**; *an-nafs al-muṭma'inna* ("the tranquil soul at peace", Q 89:27) **[H]**. *(Refinement, §9: Q 12:53 is classically read as spoken by* ***either*** *Yūsuf* ***or*** *the wife of al-ʿAzīz — the tafsīr tradition is split; the term and its meaning are unaffected.)*
- **Diseases of the heart (*amrāḍ al-qulūb*):** "in their hearts is a disease" (Q 2:10, 5:52) **[H]**; the *rust/stain* covering the heart (Q 83:14, *rāna ʿalā qulūbihim*) **[H]**; the *hardened* heart "like stones or harder" (Q 2:74) **[H]**. This is the closest Abrahamic move toward *vice-as-cognitive-blindness* (row 8).
- **Named vices:** ***kibr / istikbār*** (arrogance) — the archetypal first sin, Iblīs's *"anā khayrun minhu"* / "I am better than him" (Q 7:12), the arrogance that "refused and was arrogant" and tipped into *kufr* (Q 2:34) **[H]** *(reading of Iblīs's fall as istikbār specifically is one exegetical line, not uncontested — §9)*; ***ḥasad*** (envy, Q 113:5, 4:54) **[H]**; ***bukhl*** (miserly withholding — narrower than Latin *avaritia*; Q 3:180, 47:38) **[H]**; ***shahawāt*** (beautified worldly craving, Q 3:14) **[H]**; ***ḥubb al-ʿājila*** (love of the fleeting world, Q 75:20) **[H]**; ***hawā*** (caprice elevated to a god, Q 45:23) **[H]**.
- **Ghazālī's *muhlikāt*** (the "destroyers", *Iḥyāʾ* Rubʿ al-Muhlikāt) **[M, secondary]** — *kibr, ʿujb, ḥasad, ghaḍab, bukhl, ḥubb al-dunyā* — the nearest Islamic thing to a systematic capital-vice list, but it is 11th-c. scholarship, not Quran.

**Faculty model:** the *nafs*, seat of appetite and moral agency. The three "states" are not three souls but three *conditions of one nafs* along a developmental arc — a therapeutic staging of the ego, closer to a psychology of moral development than to a sin-catalogue.

### 3.4 Hinduism — the six enemies and the five afflictions

Two models, at different depths:

- **The Ṣaḍripu / Ariṣaḍvarga** (six enemies) — *kāma* (desire), *krodha* (anger), *lobha* (greed), *moha* (delusion), *mada* (pride/intoxication), *mātsarya* (envy). The compound term itself is *later/Purāṇic* **[M]** — *not* in the Gītā or principal Upaniṣads — but its members are deeply scriptural:
  - **The Gītā's ladder of ruin (2.62–63) [H]:** *"...saṅgāt sañjāyate kāmaḥ kāmāt krodho 'bhijāyate"* — from attachment→desire→**anger**→delusion (*sammoha*)→loss of memory→destruction of *buddhi*→ruin. **This is the single most important verse in the whole comparison:** it makes *krodha* a *downstream product of thwarted kāma*, which is exactly the CBT frustration→anger sequence.
  - **The enemy named (3.37) [H]:** *"kāma eṣa krodha eṣa rajoguṇa-samudbhavaḥ"* — it is *kāma*, it is *krodha*, born of *rajas*, "the enemy here." Its seat (3.40): the senses, mind and intellect.
  - **The three gates of hell (16.21) [H]:** *"kāmaḥ krodhas tathā lobhas"* — the Gītā's *own* short vice-list is a **triad**, not a seven.
- **Patañjali's five *kleśas* (Yoga Sūtras 2.3) [H]:** *avidyā* (ignorance), *asmitā* (I-am-ness), *rāga* (attraction), *dveṣa* (aversion), *abhiniveśa* (clinging to life). 2.4 **[H]** makes *avidyā* the *field* (*kṣetra*) of all the others; 2.5 **[H]** defines it as *mistaking the impermanent for permanent, the impure for pure, the painful for pleasant, the non-self for self.* **This is a schema running its filter on reality — the deepest model in the entire dossier.**
- **Vedic depth:** *kāma* as the primal cosmic seed of mind (Ṛg Veda 10.129.4, the Nāsadīya Sūkta) **[H]** — desire is *ontologically prior and good*; and *kāma* as the engine of karma (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.5) **[H]** — "as is your desire, so is your will; as your will, so your deed."

**Faculty model:** the *antaḥkaraṇa* as the Kaṭha Upaniṣad chariot (1.3.3–4) **[H]** — *ātman* (rider) > *buddhi* (charioteer) > *manas* (reins) > *indriyas* (horses) > *viṣayas* (roads). Passion is not a substance but a *malfunction of the stack*: the horses bolt, the reins go slack, the charioteer loses the rider.

### 3.5 Buddhism — everything is a mode of three roots, and the root of the roots is ignorance

- **The hub — three unwholesome roots / poisons (*akusala-mūla*), Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.69 [H]:** *lobha* (greed), *dosa* (hatred), *moha* (delusion). Imaged as fire/grip/net/river in Dhammapada 251 **[H]**. At the Bhavacakra's hub: cock, snake, pig — *and the pig (delusion) leads the other two* **[M, iconographic]**.
- **The broadest category — *kilesa* (defilements),** ten *kilesa-vatthūni* (Abhidhamma) **[M]**: the three roots + *māna* (conceit), *diṭṭhi* (views), *vicikicchā* (doubt), *thīna* (sloth), *uddhacca* (restlessness), *ahirika* + *anottappa* (shamelessness, recklessness).
- **The five hindrances (*pañca nīvaraṇāni*), SN 46.37 [H]:** *kāmacchanda* (sensual desire), *vyāpāda* (ill-will), *thīna-middha* (sloth-torpor), *uddhacca-kukkucca* (restlessness-worry), *vicikicchā* (doubt) — meditation obstacles, *not moral sins*.
- **The root, SN 12.2 [H]:** *avijjā* = not-knowing the Four Noble Truths, the first link of dependent origination. **Craving and aversion are downstream of it: you crave and hate because you first mis-see.**

**Faculty model:** the *citta* (mind-stream) — *not a soul* (*anattā*). AN 1.49 **[H]** calls the mind *pabhassara* (luminous), defiled by *incoming* (*āgantuka*) defilements — so purification *uncovers* rather than manufactures. A defilement lives at three depths: *anusaya* (latent), *pariyuṭṭhāna* (surging), *vītikkama* (erupting into act). **Buddhism refuses the "sin" frame twice over: no lawgiver, no guilt, no repentance — the frame is explicitly medical — and it roots all vice in ignorance, flatly contradicting Augustine's claim that pride is the root.**

### 3.6 Sikhism and Jainism — five thieves under one ego, and four sticky passions

- **Sikhism — the *Panj Chor* (five thieves) / *Panj Vikaar*:** *kaam* (lust), *krodh* (wrath), *lobh* (greed), *moh* (attachment), *ahaṅkār* (ego/pride) — enumerated in one line at Gurū Granth Sāhib **Ang 600** (M3) **[H]**: *"Eis dehi andar panch chor vaseh..."* They are *chor* (thieves) because they steal the inner *amrit*. **Above all five sits *haumai*** ("I + me"), the master disease — *"Haumai deeragh rog hai"* (ego is a chronic disease, Ang 466) **[M]**, and *"Haumai naavai naal virodh hai, dui na vaseh ik thaae"* (ego and the Name cannot share one heart, Ang 560) **[H]**. The honest structure is **5 thieves under 1 root, not 6 coordinate vices.** *(Note: the user's framing "six minus mada and matsarya plus ego" is almost right — the precise move is: keep kaam-krodh-lobh-moh, rename mada→ahaṅkār and foreground it, drop mātsarya. Envy has no Sikh slot at all.)*
- **Jainism — the four *kaṣāyas* (passions):** *krodha* (anger), *māna* (pride), *māyā* (deceit), *lobha* (greed) — *Tattvārtha Sūtra* 8.1 **[H]** names *kaṣāya* among the five causes of karmic bondage; each passion is graded into four intensities (16 total, TS 8.9–10) **[M]**. The Āgamic antidote formula (*Daśavaikālika* 8) **[M]**: *conquer anger by forbearance, pride by humility, deceit by straightforwardness, greed by contentment* — and *"lobho savva-vināsano"*, **greed destroys everything.** *(Structural note: Jain **māyā/deceit** is the one headline vice in the whole comparison with **no cognate** in the seven sins **or** in CBT — it is interpersonal-strategic, not a self-directed appraisal error. §7.)*

**Faculty models — deliberately different:** Sikh *man* (mind), diseased by *haumai*, the false belief that the self is the *doer* (*kartā*) rather than the receiver of *Hukam* (Divine Order). Jain: passions are literal **karmic adhesive** — *"sticky" (kaṣ)* substances that make karma-matter bind to the soul. These two do not reduce to each other, and neither reduces to Beck's information-processing model.

### 3.7 CBT — the surface distortions and the schema that makes them

Three nested lists get conflated; precision requires keeping them apart:

- **Beck's original six "systematic errors"** (1963 *Archives of General Psychiatry* paper; Beck, Rush, Shaw & Emery 1979) **[H, attribution]** — the scholarly root: *arbitrary inference; selective abstraction; overgeneralization; magnification/minimization; personalization; absolutistic/dichotomous thinking.*
- **Burns's ten** (*Feeling Good*, 1980) **[H, attribution; M, verbatim]** — the list most people mean: all-or-nothing; overgeneralization; mental filter; disqualifying the positive; jumping to conclusions (mind-reading + fortune-telling); magnification/catastrophizing & minimization; emotional reasoning; should-statements; labeling; personalization.
- **The pop-CBT fifteen** (McKay/Davis/Fanning 1981) — adds fairness-fallacy, control-fallacies, fallacy of change, heaven's-reward. **These four are worksheet folklore with no research base and no vice cognate** — flagged so they are not mistaken for natural kinds.

**Faculty model — four layers, deepest to shallowest:** *core beliefs / schemas* ("I am unlovable / helpless / superior") → *intermediate beliefs / conditional assumptions* → *automatic thoughts* → *distortions*. When a schema activates (under stress or loss), it selectively admits confirming data and screens disconfirming — **which is what manufactures the distortions.** A distortion is not a free-floating error; it is a schema doing its filtering job. **Empirically:** the CD-Quest (de Oliveira) factor-analyzes to a *unitary* structure (α ≈ 0.85) across Brazilian, US, Turkish, Australian and Chinese samples — validating "distorted thinking" as **one latent trait, not ten or fifteen separable ones.** *(How much metaphysical weight that single factor can bear is contested — §6.)*

### 3.8 The older and non-Abrahamic roots

- **Stoic *pathē*** (Chrysippus, via Cicero *Tusculan Disputations* IV) **[M]**: four passions on two axes (present/future × good/evil) — *epithumia* (craving a future good), *phobos* (fear of a future evil), *hēdonē* (elation at a present good), *lypē* (distress at a present evil). Envy, anger, pity are *species* filed underneath. The ruling faculty (*hēgemonikón*) *assents* to a false impression — **the direct ancestor of CBT.**
- **Aristotle** (*Nicomachean Ethics* II.6–9) **[H]**: virtue as the *mean* — the chassis Rambam and Aquinas both borrow.
- **Ancient Egypt** — the 42 Negative Confessions (*Book of the Dead*, Spell 125, Papyrus of Ani, c. 1250 BCE) **[H]**, the heart weighed against the feather of *Maʿat*. **Deliberately excluded from the parallel:** these catalogue *denied deeds*, not diagnosed inner states — a different genre. Forcing them in is a category error (§7).
- **Zoroastrianism** — the good-thought/word/deed triad (Yasna 30.3) **[H]**; *Aēshma*, the daēva of Wrath who "throws anger into the hearts of men" (Yasht) **[L]** — etymological ancestor of Asmodeus. **The one tradition that puts part of vice *outside* the will** (as Islam's Shayṭān-*waswasa* does), breaking the interiority both the Western vices and CBT assume.
- **Confucius / Mencius / Laozi** — *kèjǐ fùlǐ* ("subdue the self, return to ritual", Analects 12.1) **[H]**; Mencius's four *sprouts* (2A.6) **[H]** — the diseased-faculty model *inverted* into positive seeds; the *Daodejing* (ch. 46) **[H]** rooting all calamity in *not knowing what is enough.*

## 4. The thesis, and its demotion

**The seductive version** (and it is not nothing): these are independent empirical discoveries of one underlying structure — a mind that mistakes its own appraisals for reality and organizes behavior around a reified, defending self. And the sharpest supporting move is *his* intuition: **the count is a cultural artifact.** Eight for Evagrius, seven for Gregory (who *added* envy to get there), four for the Jains, five for the Sikhs, six for the Ṣaḍripu, ten for Burns, fifteen for the worksheets — each a culture deciding where to cut one continuous field. The CD-Quest collapsing to a single latent factor is offered as the smoking gun: when you actually *measure* the taxonomy, the ten-or-fifteen divisions dissolve into one.

**Why it gets demoted — the red team's four hits, each of which I accept:**

1. **The flagship convergence is actually diffusion.** The synthesis's paradigm case of "rediscovery, not copying" was Chrysippus↔Beck. That is false and self-refuting: Ellis built REBT *on* Epictetus, and both Ellis and Beck cite Stoicism as cognitive therapy's ancestor. The strongest "independent" case is a documented *transmission*. Once the diffusion-contaminated pairs are removed (Stoa→CBT; Evagrius→Gregory; Rambam→Aristotle; *Aēshma*→Asmodeus; the shared Indic *rāga/dveṣa/moha* vocabulary; even 20th-c. mindfulness mediating the Buddhism↔CBT resonance), **the residue of pure independent convergence is thin** — possibly just the near-tautology that frustrated desire yields anger (Gītā 2.62).
2. **It is unfalsifiable as stated.** Convergence "proves the shared object"; divergence "proves different grids cutting one seamless field." When *both agreement and disagreement* confirm the theory, no observation could disconfirm it. That is the central defect, and it is fatal to the *strong* claim.
3. **"Boundaries are cultural" does not entail "the field has no seams."** Four traditions carving 4/5/6/7/10/15 heads shows the *borders* are conventional. It does not show there is one continuous substance underneath rather than *several genuinely distinct phenomena with fuzzy edges* (juridical will-vices, karmic soul-stains, information-processing biases). That inference is a non-sequitur.
4. **The root is contested, which kills "one structure."** The two most systematic traditions name *different* roots — will (Augustine) versus intellect (Buddha / Beck). Observers who disagree about the causal topology of the thing are not describing one structure differently; they are positing *incompatible* structures. And the "where it breaks" list (§7) means the nine observers agree on maybe *three of nine rows*. A structure agreed on one-third of the time is better evidence for **family resemblance with no common essence** than for a single natural kind.

**What actually survives**, stated so it can't be inflated again: *documented lineage* (Evagrius→Cassian→Gregory→Aquinas, back through Plato and the Stoa) is real and correctly cited. *Shared Indic vocabulary* across Hindu/Buddhist/Jain/Sikh is real. *The historical deflation of "seven"* is solid and genuinely useful — it demolishes any "the Bible lists seven deadly sins" naivety. The *one clean structural convergence* is *krodha*-as-thwarted-*kāma* matching frustration→anger. And the *"the drive is good when channeled"* observations (Vedic *kāma*, the *yetzer* pronounced "very good") are correct — but they cut *against* the unity thesis, because a calibratable healthy appetite is not the same object as a sin to be extinguished. **Net: excellent as comparative religion with an honest failure-list; false as metaphysics. The correct claim is family resemblance plus one real transmission, not one discovered thing.**

## 5. Where the mapping breaks (the proof this isn't flattery)

Seven hard failures. Any one is survivable; together they are why §4 demotes the thesis.

1. **Sloth/acedia has no clean cognate** outside Christian monasticism — and wouldn't stay put even there, splitting and merging with sadness for a thousand years. Islam *reverses* the valence (*ḥuzn*/sadness can be virtuous; *kasal*/laziness is never cardinal). Hindu *viṣāda* is a *starting condition to transcend*. Buddhist *thīna-middha* lands near "sloth" *by translation accident only*. The vice CBT explains *best* (depression, via the triad) is the one the religions barely name or even valorize.
2. **Jain *māyā* (deceit) breaks all three frames** — no slot in the seven sins, no clean CBT distortion. It is the one headline vice fundamentally about how you treat *other people*, not how you mis-see reality.
3. **Envy's "universality" is a fiction** — absent from the Sikh five and Jain four, and a *late Gregorian insert* to reach seven.
4. **Delusion/ignorance is the mirror-image gap** — the *root* of the entire Indic/Buddhist analysis, with *zero* cognate in the seven sins, because the Christian scheme moralizes the will, not the intellect. Conversely, the sins' juridical core (offense against a lawgiver, guilt, forgiveness) has *no* cognate in Buddhism, Jainism, or CBT — all non-juridical.
5. **The external tempter breaks interiority** — Shayṭān's *waswasa* and the Zoroastrian daēvas who "throw" wrath locate part of vice *outside* the mind, which neither Augustine nor Beck can hold.
6. **Two traditions call the drive *good* when channeled** — Vedic *kāma* (the cosmogonic first seed), the *yetzer hara* ("very good") — which the sin-frame structurally cannot represent.
7. **The frame fails inside CBT itself** — the distortions are *many-to-many* with the vices (all-or-nothing and mind-reading each generate several sins), so nothing pairs one-to-one; and several popular distortions are research-baseless folklore. Plus: the Egyptian material doesn't belong at all (denied *deeds*, not diagnosed states).
